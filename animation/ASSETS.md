*The motion capture data used in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu. The database
was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217.*

**Why not Mixamo?** Mixamo needs an Adobe account to download, and its terms forbid
redistributing the raw character and animation files, which this repo would have to do to
ship the asset. The CC0 Quaternius libraries use a compatible humanoid rig and can be
redistributed freely. To use Mixamo anyway, export the clips as glTF, add them to
`tools/build_character.py`, and keep the files out of the repo.

### Compatibility notes
- The UAL1 mannequin is the target rig. UAL2 (Unreal-style bone names) and CMU (BVH, different
  proportions and rest pose) are **retargeted by bone direction**: each bone is turned so it
  points along the matching source bone, the hips copy the source pelvis orientation, and the
  hands copy the source hand orientation, so weapon grips keep their twist. Hip motion is scaled
  by the ratio of leg lengths.
- CMU's `Hips→LowerBack` and `Spine1→Neck` links have zero length, and its thumb joint sits on
  the wrist, so the mapping uses `Neck1` and the thumb's end site instead.
- Root motion (forward travel) is pulled out of the dash, slide and knockback clips and
  applied by the game, so walls still stop the body.
- Tested visually with `tools/preview_clips.py` (contact sheets of every clip).

## Technical references
- glTF 2.0 specification (skins, inverse bind matrices, animation samplers): <https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html>
- BVH motion format (channel order, offsets, end sites) as used by the CMU conversions
- moderngl (offscreen OpenGL 3.3 context, multisampled framebuffers): <https://moderngl.readthedocs.io>
- pygame surfaces and blending: <https://www.pygame.org/docs/ref/surface.html>
- MediaPipe Pose (landmarks and segmentation mask): <https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker>
- The fighting moves follow the recorded motion: the kicks and boxing footwork come from the
  CMU captures (real performers). Wind-ups are only slowed down, never sped up, so each strike's
  contact frame comes from the recording.
